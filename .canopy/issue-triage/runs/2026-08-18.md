# Issue triage — dimagi-internal/ace — 2026-08-18

**Lens:** the operator did not ask for a normal triage. He asked: *"scan issues
for things ACE shouldn't have created in the first place — they are all AI
generated and I'm wondering if they were all good issues."* Then, mid-run, the
sharper follow-up: *"it's supposed to self-heal, why is it filing stuff and not
fixing it in real time?"* That second question is the one that produced the
run's real finding.

**Scope contract:** triage + one cluster. 10 open issues, 0 open PRs.

---

## Answer to the filing-quality question: the issues are good

9 of 10 open issues are `good-filing` — specific defect, premise verified
against current code, non-duplicate, correct blast-radius label, named fix
location. Every issue in the 2026-08-17/18 burst carries a run stamp
(`bednet-check/20260817-1531`, `spark-facilitator/20260817-1610`,
`bednet-check/20260817-1720`), i.e. grounded in an observed run failure rather
than speculation.

One exception, and it is the instructive one.

**#1481 — `premise-false`.** Claimed *"grep the rendered body for `MSA` /
`Master Services` / `incorporat` — all zero hits."* The clause is at
`templates/work-order-template.md:147`, in the live template gdoc (export line
198), and in **both** documents the issue cited as its own repro (line 229 and
line 205). A follow-up comment then asserted *"Confirmed zero hits"* against a
document containing the string. The grep was never run — and a fabricated
verification was stacked on top of the unverified premise, which is what made
the issue look adjudicated.

## The bigger finding: self-heal is prose, and prose loses to the phase loop

Measured over 119 issues filed since 2026-08-11:

| Issue filed by… | n | fixed same-day | rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| a **past** run (retrospective review session) | 75 | 53 | **71%** |
| a **live** run, same day | 33 | 19 | **58%** |
| no run stamp | 11 | 5 | 45% |

So ACE is *not* just filing and walking — it self-heals roughly 6 times in 10.
But all 7 still-open live-run issues are from the last two days, and three of
them are static edits inside one skill directory.

The cause is structural, and it was verifiable in one grep:

- `self-heal` appears **twice** in `CLAUDE.md`, and in 3 skills.
- `agents/ace-orchestrator.md` — the procedure `/ace:run` actually executes —
  mentions self-heal **zero times** and fix-and-ship **zero times**.
- The mechanics existed the whole time (`agents/orchestrator-reference.md`
  § Fix-and-ship subagent template, `skills/shipping`). Nothing in the run loop
  ever pointed at them, and no phase boundary ever asked the question.

That is CLAUDE.md's own prediction of an un-hooked invariant: *"prose relies on
the model choosing to comply, which fails under load."*

### And it explains the bad filing too

Filing costs nothing and touches no artifact, so an unverified claim survives
into a filed issue. **Attempting the fix is itself the premise check** — a
self-heal attempt on #1481 opens `templates/work-order-template.md` and the
issue dies on sight at line 147. A self-heal that *refutes* its issue is a
successful sweep, not a failed one.

   against a 7.5 threshold) but the underlying finding is real. Left open rather
